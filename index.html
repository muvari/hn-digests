1 Health

Things to know when you get cancer

1,572 points · 378 comments · jjmaxwell4.com

A patient diagnosed with squamous cell carcinoma in his mouth shares practical lessons learned while navigating treatment. He stresses relentless follow-up on scheduling (calling back for cancellation slots), skepticism toward AI chatbots that downplayed his symptoms, pushing doctors for comprehensive genetic testing, and using NCCN standard-of-care flowcharts to understand options rather than trusting the process passively. The throughline is that patients must actively drive their own care rather than assume the system will catch everything.

What the comments said

  • Commenters thanked the author profusely, with several predicting the post will materially help other patients navigate a cancer diagnosis.
  • Many shared their own painful experiences with dismissive or under-anesthetized dental/medical care, echoing the post's "take control" message.
  • A few suggested alternative/unproven treatments (HIFU, PNC-27 peptide); others pushed back hard that untested medicine is still untested.
  • Some compared doctors' inconsistent care to software engineers' own sloppy practices, while others defended physicians as generally conscientious.
  • Several pointed to the late Jake Seliger's widely-read blog series on fighting cancer and navigating the US medical system as essential further reading.
2 Business

How Amazon pushes up prices all across the Internet

875 points · 667 comments · not-ship.com

The piece argues Amazon's marketplace fee structure — referral fees, FBA fulfillment, storage, advertising, and more — now claims close to half of many sellers' revenue, up from about 19% in 2014. Because Amazon's "fair pricing" policies penalize sellers who list items cheaper elsewhere, those inflated fees get baked into prices across the web, not just on Amazon. The author ties this directly to the FTC's pending antitrust case against the company.

What the comments said

  • Several sellers and people who've reviewed FBA books confirmed the ~50% effective cut, matching the article's numbers.
  • Many commenters report Amazon prices now routinely beat by eBay and AliExpress on everyday goods, reversing Amazon's old value proposition.
  • Commenters note Amazon's Fair Pricing Policy doesn't apply in the EU/UK (it's illegal there), and the EU is investigating the practice.
  • A counter-argument: a ~50% retail cut isn't unusual for brick-and-mortar stores either, so the framing may overstate how unique Amazon's take is.
  • Others complain that non-brand search results are now dominated by low-quality, nearly interchangeable Chinese-manufactured goods.

8 Programming

Better HTTP/1 connection reuse in Go 1.27

270 points · 45 comments · go.dev

Go 1.27's `net/http` Transport now automatically drains unread HTTP/1 response bodies in the background (up to 256 KB, capped at 50ms) when a caller closes them without reading to EOF, so the underlying TCP connection can return to the idle pool for reuse instead of being discarded. This fixes a long-standing performance trap where code that only checks status codes (or JSON decoders that stop just short of EOF) silently forced a fresh TCP+TLS handshake per request. The Go team's benchmark shows ~5x faster repeated HTTP/1 requests (710ms → 150ms for 100 requests); HTTP/2 was never affected since it multiplexes streams.

What the comments said

  • Several commenters say they can now delete long-standing hand-rolled "drain the body" helper functions copied between projects for years.
  • One concern: this changes an implicit guarantee that closing a body early skips downloading it entirely — though most agree the tradeoff (capped at 50ms) is reasonable.
  • A subtle gotcha raised: `json.Decoder` can leave a trailing newline undrained because it only reads in fixed-size chunks, silently preventing connection reuse; calling `Decoder.More()` works around it.
  • Some ask whether reverse proxies (e.g. nginx) and `Connection: close` interact safely with the new draining behavior; responses suggest edge cases remain with proxies that error on unconsumed bodies.
  • A few note this behavior isn't well documented directly on `Body.Close()`, despite being a frequent source of confusion for Go HTTP client users.

10 Data & Energy

The United Kingdom generates less electricity per person than in the 1970s

405 points · 732 comments · ourworldindata.org

Our World in Data reports that UK electricity generation per person peaked in 2003 and has since fallen by more than a third, back to levels last seen in the late 1960s, even as global per-person generation has risen over 50% this century. The piece attributes this only partly to deindustrialization: industrial electricity use fell about a quarter since 2005, but residential and commercial use fell by a similar share, and rising imports (1%→09% of demand since 2010) account for some of the drop too.

What the comments said

  • Several commenters credit efficiency gains — LED lighting replacing incandescent/CFL bulbs, better appliance standards, and PC power management — as a major residential driver.
  • Others point to high and rising UK electricity prices as simply suppressing demand, with some citing current per-unit costs as unusually expensive.
  • A counterpoint: industrial output is actually much higher in real terms than in the 1970s, complicating the simple deindustrialization story.
  • Commenters flag that incandescent bulbs' waste heat also contributed to space heating in winter, partially offsetting apparent efficiency gains seasonally.
  • The IEA sectoral split cited in the article — roughly 50/50 industrial versus residential decline — was highlighted as evidence that no single explanation dominates.

Fancy Curl Sites

131 points · 25 comments · nytpu.com

The author explains how they built a "curl-able" version of their personal site: running `curl` against their domain returns an ANSI-colored, terminal-formatted version of the page instead of raw HTML, generated by detecting curl/terminal clients and serving a hand-written ANSI-marked-up file. The idea was inspired by similar sites from other hackers, though the author built their implementation independently rather than using an existing templating library.

What the comments said

  • Multiple commenters shared their own similar curl-able personal sites and subdomains (e.g. showing currently-listening music or recent reading).
  • Discussion of the right HTTP mechanism for this: some argue content negotiation via the `Accept` header (rather than user-agent sniffing or a custom `X-Original` header) is the more standards-correct approach.
  • One commenter describes an alternative, fully static technique using terminal "alternate screen buffer" escape codes embedded directly in an HTML file, so the same URL serves both curl and browser clients without any server-side detection.
  • Someone notes it works fine on Windows too, as long as you use Windows Terminal rather than classic cmd.exe or PowerShell's aliasing of `curl` to `Invoke-WebRequest`.
  • A tangential mention: a concurrent curl/libcurl CVE (2026-92392) affecting malicious-server attacks on clients was raised as topically relevant, unrelated to the site itself.

The Consensus, a software-infrastructure newsletter, built an in-house "AI tells" detector to flag likely AI-generated submissions for its curated article feed, after repeatedly featuring pieces that read as AI-written and getting called out by readers — motivated by research showing readers strongly prefer "bad" human writing over "good" AI writing.
