The post works through how to define and compute the entropy of a Markov chain, an idea that predates stochastic thermodynamics by decades. What the comments said

  • Commenters worked out the formula together: entropy is the stationary-distribution-weighted sum over the transition matrix's entropy, i.e. −Σ v_i p_i,j log(p_i,j), after an initial mistaken guess.

  • One commenter offered a spectral view: decomposing the transition matrix into eigenvectors, each with its own entropy, to characterize how a localized initial state thermalizes toward the stationary distribution.
  • A more philosophical thread linked entropy and thermodynamics to definitions of life and death — organisms as systems that resist entropy by consuming energy from their environment.

176 points · 33 comments · mysk.blog

Goodhart's Law Comes for Every Benchmark You Trust

87 points · 36 comments · cacm.acm.org

CACM returned a 403 on both the automated fetch and a follow-up attempt, so this summary is drawn from the title and discussion. The post argues that any benchmark that becomes a widely used optimization target inevitably gets gamed, per Goodhart's Law, and that private, periodically refreshed test sets are close to the only real defense — since content that's never touched the public web can't leak into training data, and rotating the questions prevents memorization from paying off across years.

What the comments said
