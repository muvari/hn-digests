1 Business & Tech History

What Sun got wrong

611 points · 352 comments · bcantrill.dtrace.org

Oxide co-founder Bryan Cantrill reflects on what doomed Sun Microsystems, prompted by homage t-shirts his company made for an internal meetup. He argues Sun's core failure wasn't its technology or culture but that it grew "bored with the mechanics of running a business" — illustrated by a 2005 episode where a fast-growing startup running on Sun software couldn't get Sun's sales team to answer the phone, while a Dell rep made the same customer feel like a VIP overnight. That startup (which Cantrill later joined) and its Dell rep eventually became his co-founders at Oxide, and the piece frames the shirts as honoring what these defunct companies got right while learning from what they got wrong.

What the comments said

  • Many commenters with firsthand experience buying Sun/DEC hardware in the 1990s-2000s corroborated the "operational failure" thesis, describing nightmarish sales processes and pricing that made Dell an easy win.
  • Several pointed to Solaris's weak software experience — no real package manager, poor installers, a dated UI — as a parallel failure alongside the sales dysfunction, despite genuinely excellent hardware.
  • A thread debated whether any single department could be blamed for Sun's collapse; most agreed responsibility ultimately sits with executive leadership.
  • Nostalgic commenters recalled being awed by SPARC/Solaris "big iron" as students and learning Unix via cast-off Sun gear.
  • A few drew analogies to today's AI/cloud pricing, wondering if commodity competition could similarly undercut current high-margin providers.
