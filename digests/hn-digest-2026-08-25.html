Apple introduces M6 and M5 Ultra

1,111 points · 1,065 comments · apple.com

Apple announced M6, its first 2nm chip (debuting in the Mac mini), and M5 Ultra, its first quad-die chip built via next-gen UltraFusion (debuting in the Mac Studio). What the comments said

  • A tangent about Apple's use of the game 'Mixtape' in graphics benchmarks, with disagreement over whether it's a fair demanding-game showcase or just a cutscene-heavy, poorly optimized title.

7 Environment

Bomb fishing is wreaking havoc on Indonesia's coral reefs

310 points · 160 comments · e360.yale.edu

Researchers used cheap underwater audio recorders and open-source AI software to detect blast fishing in Indonesia's Spermonde Archipelago, finding an estimated 8,500+ bomb detonations per year in just a 350-square-mile area — one every 62 minutes. The bombs, made from bottles packed with explosives, kill indiscriminately and turn reefs into lifeless rubble, and are likely the leading cause of reef loss in the Coral Triangle, the world's most biodiverse marine ecosystem. Experts say the practice is driven less by pure poverty than by weak enforcement, and that because it's an acute, localized stressor (unlike bleaching or warming), reefs could actually be restored if the bombing stopped; the team hopes to deploy real-time acoustic detection networks globally.

What the comments said

  • Commenters debated the root cause: some blamed poverty and colonial/economic history for pushing fishers toward blast fishing, while the article itself notes the practice is more common among middle-income fishers who can afford boats and explosives.
  • Several people questioned the article's math on 'three football fields of reef destroyed per bomb dropped every 62 minutes,' with replies suggesting not every blast lands on reef and that explosive shockwaves in water travel far and kill broadly.
  • A recurring disagreement: some called blast fishing shortsighted idiocy, while others called it a rational, effective technique for feeding a family today, comparing enforcement-only approaches unfavorably to giving fishers alternative livelihoods.
  • A tangent thread mocked bomb fishing being framed as an 'unintended consequence,' and another criticized American units of measurement (football fields) for describing the destruction.
  • A few commenters turned philosophical, framing the story as evidence of broader human selfishness toward ecosystems, with pushback that subsistence fishers' choices aren't equivalent to corporate greed.

Training AI to Paint with Code

206 points · 24 comments · surya.website

Peppermint oil reduces blood pressure by 8.48 mmHg in small study

228 points · 108 comments · journals.plos.org

A PLOS ONE randomized, placebo-controlled trial gave 40 people with pre- or stage-1 hypertension either peppermint oil or a peppermint-flavored placebo twice daily for 20 days. The peppermint group's systolic blood pressure dropped from 130.05 to 121.97 mmHg while the placebo group was essentially flat, a baseline-adjusted between-group difference of -8.48 mmHg (95% CI -14.24 to -2.73).   • A few commenters noticed waist circumference and resting heart rate also improved more in the peppermint group and floated confounds like better diet/exercise adherence in that arm rather than a direct effect of peppermint.

17 Physics

Black hole singularity is a surface not a point

233 points · 173 comments · arxiv.org

Hamilton and McMaken argue in this paper that the common claim that a black hole's central singularity is a point is wrong: two observers who free-fall in along different angular trajectories at the same time lose causal contact with each other well before reaching the center, so the singularity is better understood as an effectively two-dimensional surface rather than a point. For rotating black holes they argue the singular surface sits at the inner horizon, where tiny perturbations trigger a mass-inflation instability that collapses it into a spacelike singular surface. They further speculate that a black hole's quantum states live on this 2D surface and coevolve unitarily, in thermodynamic equilibrium, with the trapped Hawking radiation inside the event horizon.

What the comments said

  • Several physicists in the thread said this isn't a new result but rather a correction of a popular-science oversimplification, something any GR grad student would already know.
  • At least one commenter pushed back harder, arguing the topology of the singularity isn't even a well-defined question and the paper's strong claims outrun what's actually understood.
  • Casual readers found the surface-vs-point reframing genuinely mind-bending and asked how it might connect to Hawking radiation and quantum gravity, echoing the paper's own speculation.
  • Large tangents developed around Penrose diagrams (with links to visualizations and popular-science videos), frame-dragging/time-drag around rotating black holes, and comparisons to neutron star and magnetar density.
  • One subthread wrestled with how to explain black holes to non-physicists at all, noting even physicists don't fully agree on the details and that supermassive black holes are actually not very dense.
