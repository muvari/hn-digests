6 Ad Fraud

I spent $220 on Google app ads and 60% of the installs were robots

575 points · 302 comments · dayzlegame.com

A solo indie developer describes turning on a small Google Ads campaign for his puzzle app and discovering that roughly 60% of billed "installs" were bots: devices running old, unsupported app versions installed from outside the Play Store but still reported as Play installs. Because Google's bidding algorithm optimizes toward whatever conversion event you set, the bot farm's fake installs caused it to route more spend toward that same farm in a self-reinforcing loop; his fix was to change the optimization goal to an in-app action instead of a bare install.

What the comments said

  • Many commenters said this is a widespread, well-known problem, and that Google has little incentive to fix it since it profits from the ad spend regardless of traffic quality.
  • Several shared practical mitigations: excluding known bot and data-center IP ranges in Google Ads settings, noting most fraud now runs through purchased "residential" proxies on compromised routers and smart TVs rather than obvious data-center IPs.
  • Some debated the underlying incentives: bot operators are effectively paid by Google themselves for serving or clicking ads, making bot traffic profitable independent of the advertiser being defrauded.
  • A few reiterated the standard advice that early-stage paid user-acquisition experiments are especially prone to exactly this kind of waste.

  • One commenter proposed it would make a great coding-agent benchmark: have an LLM translate an async program between languages and check whether it preserves the original semantics.

23 Physics

Cherenkov Radiation

224 points · 101 comments · iaea.org

This IAEA explainer (blocked from direct fetch, so this draws on the discussion, which quotes the piece) describes Cherenkov radiation, the blue glow seen around nuclear reactor cores, which occurs when charged particles travel through a medium like water faster than light travels in that same medium, though never faster than light in vacuum, perturbing nearby atoms' energy states as they pass.

What the comments said

  • A detailed sub-thread compared the phenomenon directly to a sonic boom: light in water travels around 200,000 km/s while fast neutrinos or charged particles can exceed that, creating an analogous shock wave of light.
  • Several picked apart the source's slightly garbled phrasing about particles moving "faster than light" without qualifying "in that medium," offering clearer rewordings.
  • A deeper physics thread discussed how photons interact with matter at the wave level: individual EM waves always propagate at c, but interference between original and re-emitted waves creates the appearance of net slower propagation.
  • Someone noted specialized telescopes like MAGIC, VERITAS, and HESS use exactly this effect to indirectly detect gamma rays by observing the Cherenkov flash they trigger in the atmosphere.

System76 announced the Thelio Mira AI, a Linux workstation aimed at local AI development, configurable with up to a 16-core AMD Ryzen 9000 CPU, 192GB DDR5 RAM, and dual NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell GPUs for up to 192GB combined GPU memory, liquid-cooled, running Pop!_OS or Ubuntu, and manufactured in the US.

Show HN: Godot and Rust based multiplexer (terminal panes and more)

88 points · 46 comments · github.com

Show HN for gpty, a terminal multiplexer built using the Godot game engine for its UI and Rust for the core, providing a tiling grid of PTY panes plus a JSON-RPC/MCP control surface so AI coding agents can spawn panes, inject text, and observe terminal output programmatically without screen-scraping a TUI. The author discloses most of the codebase, including the Godot UI and the Rust-Godot bridge, was LLM-generated.

What the comments said

  • Reaction was sharply divided: many questioned the unusual choice of a game engine for a terminal multiplexer, with some calling the combination "cursed" or overkill compared to a browser or Electron-based UI.
  • Others were bothered by the visibly AI-generated nature of the code and were surprised at how high the project ranked on the front page, with some alleging vote manipulation inflates any vibe-coded project's visibility.
  • The author engaged constructively throughout, explaining the MCP-driven concept-capture design was meant to let separate agent or automation processes react to terminal output, and acknowledging the lack of screenshots and Godot's relatively high RAM usage as known gaps.
  • A tooling debate emerged over whether Godot is even suitable for compiling down to a small non-game binary, with some defending its C++ module system as fine and others recommending lighter frameworks like Raylib, Bevy, or SDL instead.
