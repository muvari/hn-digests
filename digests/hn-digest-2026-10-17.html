1 Marine Biology

Bleached corals thrive within days when touching healthy neighbour

476 points · 103 comments · nature.com

Stanford/Hopkins Marine Station researchers found that bleached coral fragments can be nursed back to full health within days to weeks simply by physically touching a healthy fragment from the same coral colony. The healthy coral's symbiotic algae migrate across a fused tissue bridge into the bleached fragment, doubling in population every four days and restoring color and nutrition far faster than natural recovery in the wild. The effect only works between genetically identical fragments of the same individual — different colonies or species don't fuse or share symbionts. The findings, published in Science Advances, offer a potential, if limited, tool for accelerating reef recovery.

What the comments said

  • Many were excited but noted the technique only works between genetically identical clones of the same coral, a constraint several said limits real-world reef restoration — though some proposed banks of tagged coral clones as a workaround.
  • Divers with firsthand Great Barrier Reef experience pushed back on the "incredible" framing, noting that at the scale of real bleaching events there's often no healthy neighbor left nearby to touch before the reef dies.
  • A tangent on horizontal gene transfer and Conway's Game of Life drew some replies, while a commenter's unrelated pseudoscience detour was firmly shut down, with a moderator reminder to share expertise constructively rather than snark.
  • One commenter closed with a blunt, personal lament about watching the reef visibly degrade between a 2007 and 2017 visit, contrasting it with society's investment in rockets and drones instead of ecological repair.

3 Energy

Solar surpasses nuclear power globally for first time

628 points · 398 comments · pv-magazine.com

The World Nuclear Industry Status Report (WNISR) 2026 finds that global monthly solar generation first exceeded nuclear in April 2025 (wind a month earlier), and from March 2025 through mid-2026 either solar or wind outproduced nuclear every month. Solar capacity grew 27% in 2025 to 2,383 GW and wind 14% to 1,291 GW, while nuclear capacity rose just 1.5 GW and operating capacity actually declined; nuclear's share of global electricity fell to 8.8%, its lowest in 45 years. Nearly all new nuclear construction is concentrated in China and Russia, while reactors elsewhere retire faster than they're built, and the report is skeptical small modular reactors will change this trajectory soon.

What the comments said

  • A big framing debate: some called decades of solar subsidy underwhelming given it only just passed nuclear, while others countered that most global solar capacity was installed in just the last 5 years and is growing far faster than nuclear ever did.
  • Commenters clarified the generation-vs-capacity distinction, noting this report measures actual monthly output (TWh), a more meaningful comparison than the capacity figures often cited in the press.
  • Several highlighted that nearly all nuclear growth is China-driven, prompting questions about how much of the renewables surge is also China-driven (answer: enormous — China has also passed "peak coal" while still growing overall).
  • A tangent on battery storage emerged as the mechanism letting solar keep outrunning nuclear despite intermittency, with pushback that renewables remain pricier once storage/backup costs are included.
  • A few readers flagged apparent inconsistencies between capacity and "first time" framing, resolved by others pointing out capacity and generation aren't directly comparable.
