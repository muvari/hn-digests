  • The author clarifies edge-routing behavior in detail: conflicting nodes sharing a side automatically space themselves out, and truly contradictory placement statements raise an explicit error rather than silently picking a winner.
  • Requests include theme support beyond the current dark default, which the author says is a near-term priority.

Hacker Atlas is a site that categorizes and visualizes what Hacker News discussions are actually about, with a browsable topic grid, a "Connections" page showing a navigable semantic graph between topics, and pace-of-conversation statistics reportedly going back to 2020.

What the comments said

  • Several say it's a welcome antidote to front-page fatigue, giving a higher-level view of what's actually trending across HN.
  • The "Connections" graph view draws particular enthusiasm, with one commenter saying it's exactly the spatial/semantic navigation they'd hoped for and wishing it were surfaced more prominently on the homepage, plus a request for scroll-to-zoom support.
  • Requests include letting users search whether their own submissions appear in the dataset, and viewing all-time trend data rather than just a rolling 30-day window.
  • One observation: a large share of the top categories shown are various flavors of AI, prompting a suggestion to add a dedicated "AI vs. non-AI" meta-view.

Analyzing Frontier Model Progress with My Favourite Game: Prince of Persia

62 points · 43 comments · blog.priyan.in

The author repeatedly handed the original 1989 Apple II Prince of Persia assembly source to each new frontier model, asking it to port the game to C#, turning the results into an informal capability benchmark. Early attempts (Claude Opus 4.6, then OpenAI Codex) got basic tile-grid movement working but never matched the real game's frame-based animation; months later, Claude Opus 5 diagnosed that core architecture problem overnight against a real DOSBox reference and extracted genuine game data straight out of the executable, and Opus 5.5 finished the job by finding and porting the original room-drawing routine via the SDLPoP open-source disassembly project.

What the comments said

  • Many are amused that the author's prompts were themselves garbled and grammatically rough, yet later models still succeeded despite that, with some guessing at a language barrier.
  • One commenter shares a similar informal benchmark using a 2008 Asteroids programming contest, finding wildly different results between three models on the same task, including one that disassembled the game's ROM unprompted.
  • A couple of commenters object to using Prince of Persia for this kind of demo at all, without much elaboration.
  • A tangent recommends the modern Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown as excellent in its own right, distinct from this retro-porting project.
