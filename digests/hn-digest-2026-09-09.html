Shopify acquires Tailwind

1,059 points · 409 comments · tailwindcss.com

Tailwind Labs founder Adam Wathan announced that after nine years of independent operation, Tailwind CSS's creators are joining Shopify to give the framework — installed some 110 million times a week — a stable long-term home. What the comments said

  • Reactions to Shopify as acquirer were mixed — some cited its track record as a good open-source steward (pointing to Rails), while others were cynical, noting Wathan reportedly still drew ~$3M/year and calling the "struggling" framing overstated.
  • Some speculated AI-driven decline in docs traffic could push more projects to paywall documentation behind AI-agent subscriptions rather than monetizing site traffic directly.
  • A recurring worry: "sugar daddy" acquisitions of critical open-source infrastructure tend to be unstable long-term, with some predicting Tailwind eventually spins out independent again.

25 Science

Researchers Spot Fake Ancient Pottery Using the Earth's Magnetic Field

81 points · 30 comments · smithsonianmag.com

Scripps Institution researchers developed a technique to distinguish ancient from modern pottery using two layered magnetic signals baked into fired clay: a strong "thermal remanent magnetization" set during firing, and a much weaker "viscous remanent magnetization" that slowly accumulates over centuries if the object stays undisturbed. By heating samples in small increments, they found genuinely ancient pottery (1,000+ years old) requires higher temperatures to erase this weak secondary signal than modern pottery does — offering museums, archaeologists, and law enforcement a new forensic tool against forgery and looting.

What the comments said

  • Several asked whether forgers could fake the effect using magnets during construction; the researchers' own quote acknowledges publishing methods risks tipping off forgers, though commenters explained the two magnetic signals (strong/fast vs. weak/centuries-long) aren't easily replicated by simply orienting magnets during production.
  • One commenter questioned the "shifting magnetic pole" explanation as the actual mechanism, arguing each ceramic's specific Curie temperature/composition is more relevant than pole drift; others clarified the two effects are distinct phenomena.
