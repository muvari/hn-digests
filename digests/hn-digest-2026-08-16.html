Asus Bike Booster

619 points · 434 comments · asus.com

Asus unveiled the Oxiis Intelligent Bike Booster (model E250G1), a clip-on friction-drive motor that converts almost any conventional bicycle into an e-bike without modifying gears or brakes. It offers 250W rated/500W peak power, a wireless cadence sensor, incline-sensing adaptive boost, and a brake-detecting taillight, powered by a small 158Wh battery good for roughly 10–50km depending on assist mode and charging in 2 hours via 100W USB-C PD. What the comments said

  • There was some enthusiasm for how easy the retrofit looks, tempered by skepticism about the unusually small battery compared to typical ebike conversion kits.
  • A sub-thread discussed the legal status of bolt-on ebike motors across jurisdictions, generally concluding they avoid motorcycle classification as long as pedals remain functional and power/speed stay under local limits, though rules vary widely (e.g., New Jersey now requires ebike registration).

5 Databases

Asynchronous I/O in DuckDB: Work, Thread, Work

275 points · 31 comments · duckdb.org

DuckDB's upcoming v2.0 release (fall 2026) adds asynchronous I/O for reading Parquet and CSV files, targeting the increasingly common case of querying remote data (e.g., S3-backed data lakes) instead of local SSDs. Previously, synchronous reads left worker threads blocked waiting on network responses; the new design adds a separate ASYNC thread pool (up to 4x system threads, capped at 256) that keeps many byte-range fetch requests in flight via a read-ahead queue, overlapping fetching with decoding on the REGULAR worker pool. The post also describes an asynchronous memory governor that throttles read-ahead to avoid out-of-memory issues when decoding lags behind network throughput.

What the comments said

  • Commenters were broadly enthusiastic, calling it long-awaited and consistent with DuckDB's trajectory toward being a serious remote/cloud analytical engine, though one pushed back that GPU-based engines like Presto-on-GPU are still faster.
  • Several questioned the benchmark setup — a 512GB-RAM machine querying a ~22GB remote file — and the post's author replied in-thread that the larger machine was for benchmarking flexibility rather than a memory requirement, since DuckDB's memory/thread limits are configurable.
  • A technical discussion covered oversubscribing I/O threads relative to CPU cores, with commenters (including the author) agreeing this generally works fine since async threads spend most of their time parked waiting on syscalls rather than consuming CPU.
  • One commenter drew a parallel to a Rust system pairing a small Rayon compute pool with a larger Tokio async pool, reporting that pattern worked well in production too.
  • A brief side question asked about SSL/HTTPS support for remote reads.

Low-Tech Ceramic Water Filter

170 points · 43 comments · wiki.lowtechlab.org

This Low-Tech Lab wiki tutorial explains and documents how to manufacture ceramic water filters at semi-industrial scale, a design originally developed in 1981 by Dr. Fernando Mazariegos in Guatemala and now produced by 61 factories in 39 countries via the Potters for Peace network. The filters are pressed from clay mixed with a combustible material like sawdust or rice husks; firing burns out that material to leave micropores of roughly 0.6–3.0 microns that mechanically trap bacteria, protozoa, and helminths, often supplemented with a colloidal silver coating for its antibacterial effect. The guide is aimed at would-be small manufacturers and NGOs rather than home DIYers.

18 Math

A SAT Attack on Tarski's High School Algebra Problem

94 points · 36 comments · arxiv.org

This paper uses SAT solvers to resolve an open question tied to Tarski's "high school algebra problem," which asks whether every true identity of addition, multiplication, and exponentiation over positive integers follows from 11 elementary axioms. The authors prove that the smallest countermodels satisfying the axioms but violating Wilkie's known counterexample identity have exactly 12 elements, confirming a conjecture by Burris and Yeats, and they enumerate exactly 8,957,952 such 12-element countermodels up to isomorphism. Their SAT-based approach outperformed prior dedicated countermodel-search tools (Mace4, SEM), and they used LLM-assisted autoformalization to verify the result in the Lean proof assistant.

What the comments said

  • A recurring question was why subtraction is excluded from the axioms; commenters explained positive integers aren't closed under subtraction, and that restricted operation set is the whole point of Tarski's original problem.
  • One of the paper's authors joined the thread to acknowledge a prior Zenodo preprint had already established the lower bound, and detailed how their SAT encoding is far more efficient (85 seconds vs. 50 minutes for the same result).
  • Some commenters appreciated the SAT encoding technique used (auxiliary "at-most-one" variables to compare function outputs) as a broadly useful trick.
  • A clarifying subthread noted the result isn't blocked by Gödel's incompleteness theorem, since the underlying equational theory is quantifier-free and decidable, just not finitely axiomatizable.
  • Several expressed discomfort with the paper's use of an LLM to generate over 10,000 lines of Lean formalization, though the author defended it by noting Lean's type-checker verifies the bulk of it, leaving only the core definitions to review manually.
