7 Education

AI boosted homework scores, then exam scores dropped: study

295 points · 310 comments · economist.com

The Economist covers a study by David Stromberg (Stockholm University) and Victor Lei and Wu Yanhui (University of Hong Kong) tracking 27,000 Chinese students aged 12-18, about 80% of whom used AI tools like Doubao and DeepSeek for schoolwork. After six months, AI users' homework scores rose 18% and completion time dropped from 64 to 45 minutes, but the same students scored 20% below non-AI-using classmates on exams. The piece argues homework performance, which used to predict exam performance, has effectively become an inverse indicator among AI users.

What the comments said

  • Commenters highlighted that the study distinguished use patterns: students who used AI to genuinely study performed similarly to (or slightly better than) top non-AI students, while those who used it to outsource homework did much worse on exams.
  • One frequently cited detail: 81% of AI users were classified as "outsourcing" their homework, with the rate rising with more AI exposure, and someone noted the "slightly higher performing" subgroup was statistically tiny (single digits to ~20 students out of 26,000).
  • Several framed it as a general truth about learning — "the journey is the point, not the destination" — suggesting AI could help if constrained to tutor/probe understanding rather than hand over answers.
  • Others were more cynical, describing AI as an amplifier that makes strong students stronger and weak/lazy students weaker.
  • Some wanted more context on which students reach for AI, noting unequal access to tutoring, devices, and curricula shapes what "responsible use" even looks like across school systems.

Why aren't smart people happier? (2022)

257 points · 406 comments · experimental-history.com

Adam Mastroianni examines why measured intelligence barely correlates with happiness, despite intuitions that better problem-solving should produce a more satisfying life. He cites meta-analyses and a large UK dataset showing only the lowest-scoring group is slightly less happy than everyone else, plus his own analysis of 50 years of General Social Survey vocabulary-test data showing a small negative correlation between vocabulary score and self-reported happiness.   • Some discussed the double edge of high IQ — potential quality-of-life advantages offset by envy, bullying, or friction from peers.

19 Tech History

Japan tried to build an operating system for the world, the US intervened

361 points · 208 comments · xda-developers.com

  • Fun trivia surfaced: commenters claim Nintendo Joy-Cons run a micro-TRON descendant, and TRON concepts persist in electronic musical instruments.
