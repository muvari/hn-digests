PHP 8.2.35

1,048 points · 552 comments · github.com

PHP shipped 8.2.35 (alongside 8.3.36, 8.4.27, and 8.5.12) as an emergency security release fixing two critical, unauthenticated remote-code-execution vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) rooted in out-of-memory handling and stale global state. 6 Security

Eight Bits of Trust: eMudhra's CA keys cracked in milliseconds

899 points · 434 comments · scotthelme.co.uk

Researcher Scott Helme found that eight of certificate authority eMudhra's intermediate CA private keys (on the P-384 elliptic curve) were trivially small numbers like 4, 56, and 115 instead of proper 384-bit random values, making them crackable in under a second. Because the certificates looked structurally valid, anyone who examined them since April 2025 — not just an attacker who "stole" something — could have recovered the keys, and most non-browser TLS clients (curl, package managers, CI pipelines) don't check revocation or Certificate Transparency, leaving them exposed even after the keys were revoked.

What the comments said

  • Many commenters were stunned at the sheer negligence, joking about how trivially weak the keys were ("isn't 4 only 2 bits?", "not even prime!").
  • Several pushed back on theories of malice, calling it almost certainly incompetence or laziness rather than an intentional backdoor.
  • A thread noted the real danger isn't browsers, which enforce CT and are already protected, but the vast invisible ecosystem of curl-pipe-sh installers, CI/CD pipelines, and IoT updaters that never check revocation.
  • One commenter recalled a past case where a CA automatically rejected a deliberately chosen low-value key, raising the question of why eMudhra's systems didn't catch this.

26 Physics

In Hilbert space, all things are quantumly possible

142 points · 78 comments · quantamagazine.org

Quanta Magazine explains how John von Neumann's 1927 papers unified Heisenberg's matrix mechanics and Schrödinger's wave mechanics by showing both were different representations of the same underlying object — a quantum state, modeled as a vector in an abstract space called Hilbert space. Rather than tracking ordinary position, a quantum state's vector encodes the probabilities of every possible future measurement outcome, with its orientation determining which outcome is more likely.

What the comments said

  • A recurring philosophical debate concerned whether Hilbert space is "real" (the territory) or just a useful map over an underlying classical world, with commenters citing differing camps — Everettian many-worlds versus more instrumentalist readings.
  • Several enjoyed a famous, possibly satirical, von Neumann quote expressing late-life doubt about Hilbert space's physical reality, with replies noting the timing is notable given what wasn't yet known in quantum field theory.
  • A tangent used an analogy about real numbers between 0 and 1 excluding integers like 3 to argue that possibility within a space doesn't mean unconstrained possibility.
  • Lighter threads riffed on "what is a Hilbert space" jokes attributed to Hilbert himself, and on superdeterminism as a competing interpretation.
