Phyllotaxis: An audio-reactive LED display

315 points · 48 comments · jagi.studio

A hobbyist's build log for an audio-reactive LED sculpture based on phyllotaxis, the spiral pattern found in sunflowers and succulents. The author generated the pattern in Processing, turned it into physical geometry with CadQuery and a 3D printer, hand-soldered 89 addressable LEDs, and wrote an STM32-based driver that runs FFT audio analysis on an I2S microphone to make the display react to music in real time.

What the comments said

  • Some noted a resemblance to a commercial product (Voria Labs' Lumanoi), though others argued the similarity is only superficial — both just use Voronoi tessellation to map LEDs.
  • General praise for how mesmerizing the audio-reactive patterns look, and for the choice of demo music.

3 Energy & Infrastructure

How Delhi cut electricity loss from 50 to 5 percent

543 points · 299 comments · spectrum.ieee.org

An IEEE Spectrum feature traces how Delhi's power grid went from losing over 50% of its electricity to theft and obsolete equipment in 2002 to losing just 5-6% today, on par with France and Belgium. The turnaround combined utility privatization, modern grid monitoring technology, and social outreach programs — including hiring and paying local women in theft-prone neighborhoods to collect bill payments alongside financial-literacy education.

What the comments said

  • Commenters were surprised loss rates of 5-6% are considered good even in rich countries like France and Belgium, given transformation and distance losses.
  • Several shared vivid personal memories of routine blackouts and scheduled water/power "load shedding" in Delhi from the 1980s-2000s.
  • The social-outreach angle — paying women in low-income areas to collect payments — drew interest as an unusually human element of an infrastructure story.
  • A tangent compared Delhi's numbers to Singapore's near-zero grid losses, crediting underground infrastructure, predictive monitoring, and (jokingly) harsh penalties for cutting lines.
